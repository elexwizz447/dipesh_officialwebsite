Why Second-Life Batteries for Nepal

EV adoption in Nepal is rising quickly, and the first generation of packs is already approaching the end of its automotive life. A pack retired at around 70–80% of its original capacity is still useful for stationary storage, where energy density matters far less than cost per kWh. The lab studies how these packs can be safely graded, rebuilt, and deployed as low-cost backup and solar storage, instead of entering the waste stream.

Unified ANFIS-Based AVR–LFC Control

MATLAB/Simulink comparison of ANFIS and PID for combined voltage and frequency regulation in interconnected systems.

COMPLETED

WHAT: Unified ANFIS-Based AVR–LFC Control for Modern Power Systems

Developed an integrated MATLAB/Simulink framework to compare ANFIS and PID controllers for unified Automatic Voltage Regulation (AVR) and Load Frequency Control (LFC) in interconnected power systems. This work investigates how intelligent adaptive control responds to cross-coupled voltage–frequency dynamics under load fluctuations.
The MATLAB simulation environment models the generator swing dynamics, excitation and governor loop interconnection and per-unit (PU) load disturbances. Research outcomes involved nonlinear interactions between voltage and frequency control, the influence of inertia and damping on system stability, and the feasibility of ANFIS as a unified controller replacing traditional PID-only strategies.

PID and ANFIS control architecture in MATLAB

PID Controller Tuning

HOW: Modular MATLAB Simulink Framework for Control Simulation

Implemented unified AVR–LFC system dynamics including synchronous generator swing equations, governor–turbine models, exciter–AVR loops and definition of system constants.
Developed two separate control scenarios:
• Baseline tuned PID control.
• Unified ANFIS controller, trained using hybrid learning to adapt membership functions and rules based on system disturbances.
Observed effects on system voltage, frequency and control dynamics during load fluctuation scenarios and variable inertia/damping intensities, enabling sensitivity studies on settling time, overshoot, and frequency–voltage stability.

ANFIS Controller Neural Network Layering

Kp - Ki - Kd Tuned Values

RESULTS: Performance Comparison & Sensitivity Analysis

Cross-Coupling Validation
Mathematically modeled and observed how frequency deviations affect terminal voltage and how voltage disturbances propagate into frequency response.
Controller Performance in Transient Dynamics and Low-Inertia Systems
ANFIS consistently delivers faster settling times and lower overshoot with smoother damping (critical for low-inertia systems). It maintains high stability through automatic adaptation to varying inertia/damping, eliminating the manual retuning required by PID.
Unified Controller Feasibility
Results demonstrate that a single ANFIS controller, complemented by P and I feedback (used to feed the change in speed into the LFC loop), can effectively regulate both AVR and LFC simultaneously. Although the cross-coupled effect is relatively small, it still exists, and modeling it highlights the novelty of this work.

Grid-Forming Inverters in PV–Hydro Microgrids

Undergraduate thesis — virtual synchronous machine control for low-inertia solar and hydro microgrids.

THESIS · ONGOING

WHAT: Understanding Grid-Forming Inverters in PV–Hydro Microgrids

This thesis explores how Grid-Forming (GFM) inverters and Virtual Synchronous Machine (VSM) control can support low inertia systems. For study purposes a solar PV and low-inertia hydro generator has been considered. Many remote microgrids struggle with fast frequency changes and unstable voltage because the hydro unit does not have enough rotating mass to absorb disturbances. This work focuses on how a GFM inverter can act as the main voltage and frequency source, and how virtual inertia can make the system behave more like a traditional generator-based grid.

The study looks at the behavior of the combined PV–Hydro–GFM system under realistic situations such as sudden load steps, cloud-driven PV changes, and weak-grid conditions.

Microgrid Overview

Ride-through curve and PV–Hydro integration model

HOW: Simulation & Modeling Approach (Ongoing Thesis Work)

Developing a dynamic model of the PV–Hydro microgrid, including a simplified swing equation to capture how virtual inertia interacts with the hydro generator's real inertia.
Building a simulation environment where the GFM inverter forms voltage and frequency, while the hydro generator synchronizes to it and the PV inverter acts as a grid-following source.
Studying how the system reacts to disturbances such as PV fluctuations, load changes, and variations in hydro output, comparing GFM operation against traditional grid-following control.
Since this work is part of an ongoing thesis, the models, tuning methods, and simulation cases are being expanded and refined step-by-step.

GFM–VSM controller design and layer structure

Proposed VSM Layout

RESULTS: Key Findings & Early Technical Insights

Improved Frequency Behavior
Early simulations show that the GFM–VSM inverter provides fast support during sudden load or PV changes, reducing frequency dips and keeping the microgrid more stable than the hydro unit alone.
Better Coordination With the Hydro Generator
When the inverter forms the grid, the hydro generator synchronizes more smoothly, experiences fewer oscillations, and avoids the "hunting" behavior seen in low-inertia setups.
Ride-Through Performance
The PV inverter follows IEEE 2800 ride-through standards, remaining connected during voltage and frequency disturbances within limits. Initial simulations show that the controller continuously supplies the reference current.

AI-Integrated Home Automation System

ESP32 hardware with an MQTT telemetry loop and a server-side learning model — two national hackathon wins.

PROJECT · COMPLETED

COMPLETE WORKING STRUCTURE (AI-INTEGRATED HOME AUTOMATION SYSTEM)

View Flowchart

• Built a working AI-enabled home automation system using an ESP32, relay module, and sensors — capable of remote control and AI-driven local automation.

• ESP32 performs lightweight inference with coefficients pushed OTA; heavy preprocessing and retraining occur on a server to improve accuracy over time.

• CADIO/cloud used for user setup and voice integration (Google Home / Alexa). Manual physical override always has local priority and is logged for retraining.

HARDWARE PROTOTYPE AND DATA COLLECTION

Market ready Device — Internal Components/Electronics

• ESP32 reads sensors (occupancy, temperature, light) and publishes telemetry via MQTT to the AI server.

• Initial dataset captured via Arduino + PLX-DAQ and used for logistic regression training and validation.

• Manual override events are recorded to refine user-preference models during retraining.

REAL-TIME CONTROL LOOP & AI (Data → Decision → Actuation)

National Hackathon Win on Project

• ESP32 publishes telemetry to telemetry/{device_id}; the AI server ingests, preprocesses, and runs logistic regression to compute probability p.

• Decision logic applies hysteresis and checks manual overrides; final commands published to cmd/{device_id}.

• Final ON/OFF decision is published back to the ESP32 and relay, then end-device actuation is achieved.

PHASES OF DEVELOPMENT

For demonstration and evaluation, we used a hybrid control architecture. The CADIO cloud / Google Home–based direct-control pipeline (User App / CADIO Cloud → MQTT Command → ESP32 → Safety / Override Check → Relay → Output) was tested successfully on real hardware and provided reliable manual and voice-driven operation. The next phase introduced the AI-driven autonomous loop (Sensors → ESP32 → MQTT Telemetry → AI Server → Logistic Regression → Decision Logic → MQTT Command → ESP32 → Relay → Output) which added a full automation layer on top of the traditional control system.

Control Architecture

• Sample telemetry: {"device":"room1_esp32","ts":"2025-11-21T12:00:00Z","occupancy":1,"temp":24.6}

• Command format: {"cmd":"SET","device":"room1_esp32","action":"ON","confidence":0.87}

• Limitation: although the AI layer enabled free automation, some AI actions still conflicted with manual overrides, even with a safety layer prioritizing user commands. This can be improved by adopting stronger preference-learning and context-aware models.
